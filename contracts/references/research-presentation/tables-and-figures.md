# Show the comparison the question requires

Choose a display that lets the researcher judge the planned scientific comparison directly. Put competing methods or conditions beside one another. Name the actual recorded quantity in table headings, figure axes, legends and captions, with its target or reference, units, denominator, direction, tested conditions and uncertainty where needed for interpretation. Use standard statistical or machine-learning terms only when the recorded definition supports them; the same measure keeps the same meaning in the account and display. A generic label such as score or quality cannot substitute for an available measure definition. Keep exact source links. Preserve unavailable values, zeroes, unsuccessful attempts and scientifically meaningful constant results. Move common metadata to a caption only when interpretation remains clear.

Match displayed precision to the comparison and uncertainty; exact values remain in saved outputs. Preserve enough precision near a decision threshold to retain the comparison. Label approximations and round bounds conservatively outward; a point estimate or ordinary sampling interval is not automatically a certified upper bound. Prefer concise mathematical notation for quantitative column headings when the recorded quantity supports it, for example $\operatorname{MSE}(\hat f)$ or $\operatorname{SD}(\hat V)$ using the supplied estimator symbols. Keep Condition and Method descriptive. Use established project notation; define any local summary operator, such as a bar for a mean across replications, in the caption. Spell out the measure name and explain its target, averaging unit or selected subset in the caption or adjacent account. Define unfamiliar symbols beside the table; a notation reference can supplement that explanation. State shared units and parenthetical uncertainty once in the caption rather than repeating a sentence in each heading. Keep empirical standard deviation, the standard error of an estimator and Monte Carlo standard error distinct; SD must not label a standard error. This changes labels, not the statistic, source keys, rows or values. Define the error or loss and averaging unit in the result account or caption. An unspecified 'mean error' cannot be relabeled as bias, mean absolute error or root mean squared error, or interpreted as lower-is-better without its definition. Keep confidence intervals and numerical reference enclosures distinct. Paired contrasts need the saved paired difference and its uncertainty; separate marginal estimates cannot supply that comparison. For selective outputs, use the same stated subset, its count and uncertainty; marginal means over different subsets with estimates or selected policies establish neither that contrast nor unconditional superiority. Never calculate missing support merely to complete a display.

Translate stored field labels into the scientific event or statistic they encode. For example, suppose a supplied offline-policy study defines $p(\pi)=\Pr_\pi(G_C>b)$ and $V(\pi)$ as expected reward, and its rows count independent training datasets. Keep headings to the quantity and a useful formula; give units, shared Monte Carlo standard error notation and conditioning once in the caption. Put measures in rows when long columns obscure the comparison:

| Stored label | Scientific heading | Meaning to explain in the caption |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `returned_per_plan` | $n_{\mathrm{sel}}/B$ | Datasets with a selected policy: $n_{\mathrm{sel}}$ counts eligible selections and $B$ counts attempted datasets; explain the supplied eligibility rule. |
| `target mean` or risk among returns | $\overline{p(\hat\pi)}$ | Cost-exceedance probability: the bar averages $\Pr_{\hat\pi}(G_C>b)$ over datasets with a selected policy; identify the cost, threshold and new-participant population. |
| `joint returns` | $n_{\mathrm{pair}}/B$ | $n_{\mathrm{pair}}$ counts the same datasets with both named policies selected; $B$ counts attempted datasets. |
| `paired score difference` | $\overline{\Delta V}$ | The bar averages the paired policy-value difference $\Delta V=V(\hat\pi_{\mathrm{mix}})-V(\hat\pi_{\mathrm{single}})$ over that matched subset, with reward units and its recorded uncertainty. |

These headings depend on the supplied definitions, not on the stored names alone. In reinforcement learning, return conventionally denotes cumulative reward; describe selection as selecting a policy, so a reader can distinguish it from policy return. Empirical confidence-interval coverage is the proportion of intervals containing their target across replications; policy-selection frequency is the proportion of datasets with a selected policy. Use each term for the event actually measured.

## Tables

Use the display and artifact formats already owned by this invocation. A main table should expose the comparison directly rather than requiring the reader to reconstruct it from nested diagnostics. Retain the complete supporting evidence and exact source links. Formatting or selecting a display does not authorize new calculations or replacement evidence.

Related exact questions may share a table or figure with complete case/metric mappings. Each exhibit answers a clear comparison and has one primary display home, with links elsewhere. In P4, each topic lists every interpreted exhibit in its existing exhibit_ids, including shared exhibits; the first coverage entry declaring it owns the display and later entries show a link. Put the intended primary entry first; keep exhibit IDs and placement instructions out of prose. There is no table-per-question or exhibit quota. A table and plot of the same data are useful together only when they serve different reading purposes. Split a dense table into complementary panels when this makes the comparison easier to judge.

Before writing a reader-facing table, decide what changes from row to row. State a fixed design choice once in the title, caption or labeled panel for the rows it describes. For example, if every row compares the mixture with an eligible singleton, name that reward difference and its direction once. Show condition, evaluation size, paired count, estimated difference and Monte Carlo standard error in the body. If a table compares several policy pairs, identify each pair where it changes. A preview can share one contrast even when the full table has five.

When scenarios or methods vary, use their descriptive names from the protocol. Keep the original row keys and full measurements in the supporting artifact. Repeated results can be the finding: retain zero events, unavailable estimates, measured uncertainty and the denominators needed to interpret them. Decide what is shared from the study design and the population for those rows. Matching rounded values alone establish neither a common setting nor a common denominator.

Select the scientific comparison before choosing columns. Put a relevant estimate and its supported comparison together when their subsets agree; label different subsets explicitly. Related values may share an ordinary scalar cell when their meanings stay explicit: estimate with its interval, count with its attempted denominator, or interval endpoints. If supplied definitions and saved rows establish that a mean uses the same population and count as an existing estimate-availability or policy-selection count column, refer to that column rather than repeat it; equal counts alone are insufficient. A shared bound can go in the caption only with its confidence level and exact applicable row family, preserving distinctions from other intervals. Keep zero outcomes, missing or inapplicable values and differing denominators visible. A caption supplies genuinely common settings, accounting facts, needed definitions, scope and exact sources without repeating the account's findings and comparator explanation. Retain the complete supporting table and mappings. In P4, the Analyst produces these artifacts from recorded results before Lead selection; the Lead cannot repair a wide table by inventing replacement cells. Keep pilot and finite code-check rows separate from the main repeated-simulation comparison unless those checks are the scientific subject.

Keep scientific dataset size, independent dataset repetitions, internal resamples and computational-check draws separate. A lottery check with many draws does not increase a scientific dataset's sample size or the number of independent datasets.

Use these patterns to choose what the reader needs. Put fixed settings in the caption even when the list below includes them:

| Question | Useful columns |
| --- | --- |
| Implementation fidelity | Check; independent reference; discrepancy; tolerance; actual outcome |
| Estimation | Scenario; target/unit; method; independent datasets attempted/usable; bias; empirical standard deviation; root mean squared error or the declared error measure; Monte Carlo standard error or other specified uncertainty; failures of the complete estimation method |
| Prediction | Dataset/scenario; target/horizon; method; split; held-out metric/unit; uncertainty; usable units |
| Interval estimation | Scenario; target; method; nominal confidence level; empirical coverage rate with its stated denominator; interval length; interval availability when relevant; applicable uncertainty |
| Constrained estimation or decision | Condition; method; scientific objective and constraint; proportion of datasets yielding an eligible estimate or selected policy, with eligibility defined; measured quantities with their units, conditioning and uncertainty; relevant measured computational resource |
| Computational cost | Scenario/size; method; repetitions; elapsed time or processor time with units and measured operations; memory use when recorded |
| Tuning | Allowed settings and rule; observed selections; actually tested sensitivity; target metric/uncertainty; usable repetitions/failures |
| Robustness | Changed data-generating or observation process; method; target metric/uncertainty; usable repetitions/failures; consequence |

A one-dataset error is not bias or root mean squared error across independent datasets. Bootstrap resamples are not additional independently generated datasets. Separate failures of the complete estimation method from failed internal fits, counts conditional on an estimate being available or a policy being selected and unstarted work. Under computational cost, report the recorded resource precisely: elapsed time, processor time and memory use are not interchangeable. State whether timing includes fitting, tuning, prediction or assessment; do not label a duration merely 'procedure time' or imply that seconds measure every computational resource. Keep these measurements out of the notation table. Add only nonoverlapping timing components. Report convergence only when recorded; selected settings alone do not establish sensitivity. No actual data means no invented actual-data table.

For constrained estimation or decision procedures, distinguish estimate availability or policy selection, constraint behavior and the scientific objective when relevant. Identify the population or selected subset for each quantity, including whether interval coverage is conditional on interval availability. Without an estimate or selected policy, its corresponding performance measure is undefined, not zero; do not invent a deployment fallback. Distinguish a certificate or bound from independently justified reference truth and its error. Omit unavailable or inapplicable columns rather than require a full-risk reference. A reference enclosure crossing a threshold leaves classification unresolved; an estimated reference also retains its statistical uncertainty. Keep failed and unknown classifications visible rather than silently counting them as safe. Preserve meaningful constant results, zero policy selections and unavailable estimates: a zero alone does not prove impossibility, and an ideal analytical feasibility screen is not observed power, estimate availability or policy-selection frequency.

## Figures and graphs

Use a figure when a pattern or relationship becomes easier to understand: error or coverage versus size, runtime scaling, assessed tuning sensitivity, a fitted function against truth, or observed-data diagnostics. A small table may fully answer the question; there is no plot quota. Define curves, axes, horizons, units, repetitions and uncertainty. Link verified underlying data through the existing exact source fields. Do not fabricate a curve from prose or infer data links from filenames.

Preserve authored graph semantics, including edge direction, conditions and unresolved dependencies. Layout is not evidence for an edge. Display reading and selection do not authorize new computations. Preserve existing fidelity checks, exact-value permissions and source authority when re-expressing an admitted display.
