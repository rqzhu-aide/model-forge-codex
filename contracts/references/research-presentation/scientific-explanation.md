# Explain the scientific object

Use the terminology of statistical and machine-learning papers to name the supplied scientific objects and measured quantities precisely, then explain their meaning in the study. Identify the actual estimand, estimator, objective, constraint or performance measure where it is needed to interpret a claim. A general word such as performance, quality, error or cost needs the specific quantity behind it. Preserve the supplied definition, scale and averaging rule across accounts. Renaming a recorded mean episode cost as mean squared error or regret requires its definition to establish that equivalence. Use terms such as bias, coverage or regret only when their defining target, reference and experiment support that interpretation. If a definition is missing, state that limitation rather than infer a familiar metric. This adds no metric or computation requirement. Translate workflow outcomes into the scientific event they represent: a policy-selection frequency is the proportion of datasets selecting an eligible policy, while reinforcement-learning return is cumulative reward. The tables-and-figures reference illustrates this translation with source-defined quantities.

Explain the target, how the method works and what its quantities mean in this problem. Use the researcher's stated goal and supplied setting; do not replace the target with an easier comparison or infer a domain meaning for an undefined quantity.

Give the defining relationship, criterion or rule needed to understand the mechanism. Distinguish the target, estimator and estimate; assumed and fitted quantities; oracle and feasible procedures; and information available before a decision versus information observed later. A target equation or list of symbols alone does not explain a method. Keep the complete canonical definition in its owned source.

For an experiment, explain the data-generating and observation processes, what one independent replication contains, fixed and varied settings, and what each comparator is allowed to know. Define measures and their averaging subsets before interpreting results. State whether the data are synthetic, observed or a computational check. An illustrative setting is not a confirmed study fact.

In P4, compose the primary account as a compact numerical experiments section. Primary setup describes data generation, observations, independent replication, comparators and measures; code-specific agreement and engineering checks remain in the protocol or implementation reader unless essential to the comparison. Select the principal patterns, negative findings and uncertainty beside their exhibits. For a hypothetical prediction study of mass measured in kilograms, when saved paired results support it: "Across 100 independent synthetic datasets, the proposed predictor reduced mean squared prediction error by 0.30 kilograms squared relative to the comparator, with Monte Carlo standard error 0.06 kilograms squared." Explain the shared training/test design and averaging rule before that comparison, and keep its tested scope and decisive qualification beside it. Different returned subsets need their own counts and do not support a paired comparison merely because both have a mean. This is a writing example, not evidence or a required design. Keep pilots and finite code checks distinct from principal repeated-simulation results. Explain a negative finding in the principal result account through a recorded diagnostic or established analytical relation when available; otherwise leave its cause unresolved. A limitation states the evidence or claim boundary.

For a targeted discussion, answer the selected question first, then give the mathematical comparison and qualifications needed to understand it. Stop when that question is answered; use exact sources for additional derivation and offer deeper explanation when useful. Do not replay the full method, research history or every possible caveat in each reply.

## Mathematical notation

Define essential symbols at first use in each independently readable account, including conditioning, units, population and denominator when they change interpretation. Pair mathematics with its scientific meaning. Use proper Markdown/LaTeX; align long relations rather than compressing them into unreadable lines.

Reuse the frozen notation admitted for the account being written. The same quantity in the same scope keeps its adopted symbol; independent candidate, paper and proof scopes may use the same letter for different quantities. Preserve source-local notation and explain any genuine translation without rewriting sealed sources. Define necessary new symbols in the scientific field that owns their mathematics; the synthesizing Lead adopts shared additions in its existing output. A changed mathematical object requires scientific revision, not a renamed notation entry. A discussion or explanation cannot publish a definition.

Reserve notation for mathematical symbols used substantively in the account, with their mathematical definition, scientific meaning and units or domain where relevant. Performance-measure labels and abbreviations belong beside results even when typeset mathematically. A substantive population risk functional remains a model quantity. Keep software terms and computational cost outside notation. Do not invent symbols to turn ordinary labels into notation, or construct a historical glossary by guessing from prose. Essential meanings still appear at first use; the lookup supports the explanation.

Use complete descriptive method names grounded in the canonical definition and supplied literature. The name should identify the actual statistical framework, estimator or algorithm and any necessary defining variant; a desired benefit or coined acronym does not explain a construction. Keep an existing method's identity and scientific name consistent across accounts rather than silently renaming it. Write performance and uncertainty measures in full in explanatory prose, for example Monte Carlo standard error rather than MCSE. Quantitative table headings should use concise mathematical notation when the recorded quantity and supplied symbols make it clear, such as $\operatorname{MSE}(\hat f)$ or $\operatorname{SD}(\hat V)$. Expand abbreviated measures and explain local symbols, averaging subsets and uncertainty in the caption or adjacent account. Standard deviation and standard errors are different quantities; concise notation must preserve that distinction. An expanded abbreviation alone does not define an ambiguous measure: state the actual supplied quantity and averaging rule.

Keep distinct quantities distinct. A scientific risk target, implemented safety bound, numerical reference enclosure and repeated-experiment uncertainty answer different questions. Preserve strict versus non-strict inequalities and equality at a threshold. No returned output, an unsafe output and an unstarted procedure are different outcomes; an undefined quantity is not zero.

An explanation establishes meaning, not validity. Use the claims-and-evidence reference for what the available support permits. Explanation depth follows the reading purpose; these scientific accounts have no arbitrary word or equation quota.
