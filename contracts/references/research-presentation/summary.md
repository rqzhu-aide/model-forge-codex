# Select the account the researcher needs first

Begin with the consequential finding and its boundary for the stated research goal. Name the target or measured quantity that makes the finding interpretable, using the same statistical definition as its source. A statement that a method is better should specify the observed measure and comparator; if the measure is undefined or the comparison unavailable, keep that limitation explicit. Then give the short method recap, what was developed and tested, and the uncertainty or missing evidence that affects the next decision. Successful execution alone is not a scientific conclusion. An honest inconclusive result can be the useful answer.

Select the primary summary rather than compressing the whole manuscript into it. Usually three short paragraphs plus needed mathematics are enough: the result and its interpretation; the decisive conditions and limitation; the next resolving question and why it matters. When a mathematical result is central, display its decisive relation and define the essential quantities locally. Keep qualifications that change the claim beside it and name the decisive assumption classes, with a link to the complete premises. Stop after these points are answered. Link the full method, proof mechanism, condition inventory and answer-routing history instead of appending them. This is a selection guide, not a word cap or permission to truncate scientific meaning.

Give each topic one primary account that answers the reader's question. Keep the supported conclusion, defining mechanism, evidence and consequential uncertainty together where they are needed. Put production details and exact identities in sources or structured fields. Link to an existing answer owner rather than creating another place to answer the same question.

In P4, Summary selects the principal finding for the scientific purpose, whether a comparison, return/failure rate or inconclusive result, with useful magnitude and uncertainty. Include a tested failure or no-useful-output condition when it changes the practical assessment, and the condition that most limits interpretation. Select what changes that assessment rather than every metric. The short method recap gives the target and main mechanism around its essential equation; the complete procedure stays in its source. Experiments and Results introduces design and measures, then selects principal patterns, negative findings and uncertainty beside the exhibits. Use the same study's conditions together when they answer one question. Existing coverage summaries own the design or principal result and its source-backed explanation; limitation fields state evidence or claim boundaries, and implication fields add the consequence after the exhibit. Per-test reviews retain required assessments and unique qualifications without retelling that section. A brief truthful statement can cover an unassessed topic; a material unassessed claim or observed failure remains conspicuous.

Explain what was assessed and what remains unassessed or inapplicable, with a reason where it affects the conclusion. Keep empirical findings separate from mathematical support, assumed conditions and intended benefits. A summary selects the relevant account; it does not require additional research to fill a topic.

Use the existing source and exhibit references for material selected within this invocation's authority. Give each exhibit one primary display home with links elsewhere. In P4, list every exhibit interpreted by a topic in that topic's existing exhibit_ids, including shared exhibits. The first coverage entry declaring it owns the display; later entries show a link. Put the intended primary entry first and keep exhibit IDs and placement instructions out of prose. A decisive number may usefully appear in both the conclusion and its table. Avoid copying the same table or full account into multiple summaries, reviews and decision contexts. Required fields remain complete: a concise implication or attributed concurrence may link to the primary account without retelling it.

Explain what an independent audit changes; keep unique qualifications and unresolved disagreement visible and attributed. Do not imply that a reviewer assessed later author revisions or material outside the submitted review scope.

Use the scientific-explanation reference for formulation and notation, claims-and-evidence for support, tables-and-figures for displays and user-inquiry for consequential requests. Full derivations, detailed interpretation, exact sources and original answers remain accessible. This guide selects and places the account; it does not create evidence, alter the target or change phase authority.
